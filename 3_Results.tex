\subsection{Dataset Analysis}
The data for this work was collected as described in Section \ref{sec:methods:dataset}, resulting in a dataset containing 90 colon biopsy samples, their associated H\&E stained images, their FTIR spectral images with absorption intensities at 801 wavenumbers from $1000$-$1800$ cm$^{-1}$, and their histopathological classification as either normal, hyperplastic, adenomatous or cancerous tissue \cite{nallala_high-resolution_2016}. Notably, this histopathological classification was made according to the most severe tissue condition present within the sample, so a sample containing only 2-3\% cancerous tissue would still be classified as cancerous. We therefore expect many individual pixels within the spectral images to be incorrectly labelled. \\

\noindent
False-colour images for samples were generated by taking the intensities for each pixel at high-variance wavenumbers $1080$cm$^{-1}$, $1230$cm$^{-1}$ and $1600$cm$^{-1}$, and then scaling them to values between $0$ and $1$, centred around $0.5$ according to the standard deviation of intensity values at that wavenumber throughout the sample. For each classification, one of these false-colour images along with its associated H\&E stain is presented in Figure \ref{fig:IR_and_HE_categories}. This figure both demonstrates the boundary of stroma regions and the locations of H\&E image folds preventing perfect alignment. Only the non-stroma regions are used for subsequent prediction analysis. Furthermore, comparisons between the intensities and variances with wavelength for each classification are shown in Figure \ref{fig:dataset_differences}.

\subsection{Spectral Matching Methods}
The most intuitive approach for classifying spectra according to their severity is to find reference spectra for each classification and then compute which reference spectra most closely resembles a test spectrum. Similarly to work by Villazon et al \cite{villazon_cancer_2025}, this work implemented a mean square error (MSE) method, a cosine similarity method. The mean square error is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MSE}(x,y) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - y_i)^2
\end{equation}
where $x$ and $y$ are the two spectra being compared, and $n$ is the number of wavenumbers in each spectrum. The cosine similarity is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Cosine Similarity}(x,y) = \frac{x \cdot y}{\|x\| \|y\|}. 
\end{equation}
By then adding $1$ to the cosine similarity and dividing by $2$, it is scaled to lie between $0$ and $1$ with $0$ denoting complete orthogonality and $1$ denoting complete similarity.
